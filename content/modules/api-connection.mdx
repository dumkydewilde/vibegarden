## What it is

An API connection is your project talking to somebody else's service: fetching the weather, reading your calendar, posting to a channel, asking a model for a summary. You send a request with your key in a header, you get JSON back, and your project does something with it. Your assistant writes that code in a minute; the parts worth your attention are where the key lives and what happens when the service says no.

## When to use it

- **The data you need already exists somewhere with an API**: a bank export, a calendar, a shop, a weather service.
- **You want to push results out** to Slack, email, a sheet, or a channel where people already look.
- **You need something you cannot build**, which for most workshop projects means a model provider, a maps service, or transcription.

If the service has no API, do not force it: an export you download by hand, or a [CSV file](/garden/modules/csv-file), is a perfectly respectable start. If the thing you want to call is an AI assistant rather than a program, the modern equivalent is MCP, covered in [What is an API? What is MCP?](/learning/what-is-an-api-what-is-mcp).

## Setup steps

1. Find the docs page with a complete example request, and get a key from the service's dashboard. Give the key the narrowest permissions on offer.
2. Put the key in an environment variable or your host's secret store. Never in the page, never in git.
3. Make one real call on its own, from a terminal or a five-line script, and look at the response before writing anything else. The actual shape is often not what the docs implied.
4. Paste that docs example into your assistant along with what you want, and let it write the real call. Without the example it will invent an endpoint that sounds right.
5. Decide what happens when it fails: retry with a growing pause on `429` and `5xx`, show a clear message otherwise, and cache anything you would otherwise ask twice.

## Options and costs

The connection itself is free. What costs money is what you call: model providers bill per request, most other services have a free tier that comfortably covers a workshop project, and rate limits rather than price are usually what you hit first.

One structural cost worth knowing: a key means your project needs somewhere to keep it, so a purely static site grows one small dynamic function. That is a fifteen-minute change, described in [Hosting your app](/learning/hosting-your-app), and [Calling an API for real](/learning/calling-an-api) covers the keys, status codes, and rate limits in full.
